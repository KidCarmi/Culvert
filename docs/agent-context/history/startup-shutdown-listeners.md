## The shutdown sequence is BOUNDED end to end, in three phases with a durability RESERVE (CHAOS-56, `main_shutdown.go` + `runtime_shutdown.go`)

[Original lines 177–177](https://github.com/KidCarmi/Culvert/blob/3fcc07e7ab5d1e3bd4d19e6cadc8bb63b682f3af/CLAUDE.md#L177-L177) · `claude-main-L177-L177`

<!-- BEGIN preserved-block: claude-main-L177-L177 -->
- **The shutdown sequence is BOUNDED end to end, in three phases with a durability RESERVE (CHAOS-56, `main_shutdown.go` + `runtime_shutdown.go`)**: SIGTERM runs 17 ordered hooks, and until CHAOS-56 the sequence was bounded only by the container's `stop_grace_period`. Three of Culvert's per-request sinks are ASYNCHRONOUS by design (`internal/logsink`, `internal/reqlog`, `internal/syslog`), and each header documents the same residual — an abrupt death loses the in-flight batch, the ORDERLY path flushes — so three durability contracts were delegated to a sequence that could not guarantee it reached the flush. **The unbounded hook is gRPC `GracefulStop`, and NOT for the reason it looks like.** grpc-go bounds the IDLE case with a 5s GOAWAY ping-ack timer (measured 6.005s end to end on v1.83.1 against a silent HTTP/2 peer — the "half-open peer waits out the ~15-minute TCP retransmit budget" story is WRONG, and measuring it is what found the real one), but `outgoingGoAwayHandler` closes the transport only when `len(t.activeStreams) == 0`; with a live stream it arms NO timer, and `GracefulStop` blocks in `for len(s.conns) != 0`. Two ordinary faults produce a stream that never finishes and neither surfaces an error: a handler blocked in `write(2)` on a wedged volume (`Enroll`/`RenewCert` persist, `PushAuditEvents` appends to a `RotatingFile`) and a peer that stops reading a large `GetConfig` response (TCP zero-window persist retries indefinitely). Reaching the grace means SIGKILL, which skips the cluster-store flush, the request-log queue drain, a clean badger close (an unclean one is exactly the torn `MANIFEST` CHAOS-50's boot-path quarantine exists for — a hung shutdown MANUFACTURES the corruption the previous sweep had to survive) and the log-sink flush **holding the lines that say why**. Six rules now hold. **(1) Three phases, and the FLUSH reserve is carved out UP FRONT** — `shutdownFlushBoundary` (105) splits the late registry via `partitionAt` into DRAIN hooks (best-effort; abandoning one costs a client retry) and FLUSH hooks (durable; abandoning one costs durability or a quarantined store), and the reserve is measured from the START of the flush phase, so a drain that overran its own share still cannot spend it. **(2) Every phase carries a REAL deadline** (`defaultShutdownBudget`: Total 45s, Early 12s, Flush 10s) — the early phase used to run under `context.Background()`, pinned as correct by a test that was pinning the DEFECT (`TestRunShutdownSequence_EarlyCtxHasNoDeadline_LateCtxDoes`, since inverted), and the late ctx was ADVISORY: `RunAll` never consulted it and most hooks cannot (`syslog`/`badger`/`logstore`/`reqlog`/`audit`/`logCloser` take no ctx; the tunnel drain took one and ran its own timer its comment calls "independent of the parent ctx", so the real worst case was 30s + 15s + the closers against a documented 30s). **(3) Every hook runs under a WATCHDOG, and the RESERVE IS RECURSIVE** (`hookBudget`): a hook may take what is left of its phase MINUS `shutdownHookMinSlice` (1s) for every hook still BEHIND it, then one shared `shutdownHookGrace` (3s) before it is abandoned, so the envelope stays `Total + 2×grace` = 51s, inside the 60s compose grace and pinned to it by a cross-artifact gate that parses `docker-compose.yml`. **The grace is SHARED BY THE PHASE, and only a clamp makes that true** (SEC-SHUT-1, `2026-09-04` review): `RunAll` folds it into ONE phase HORIZON (`phaseEnd + grace`) and `hookBudget` measures every hook's slice against THAT, so the phase can never cross it (a slice is always <= the time remaining to it) while every hook still gets its own non-zero slice. Merely CLAMPING the abandon instant is not enough and was the first, rejected shape: it bounded the total but handed a later hook an ALREADY-EXPIRED watchdog, so it could be abandoned before its goroutine entered `h.stop` — and since `main` exits as soon as the sequence returns, `request-log-close`/`audit-log-close`/`log-closer` then never ran at all (measured: a ZERO-length window for flush hooks 4-6), starving exactly the closers the reserve protects (Codex P1, PR #1311). It was first charged PER HOOK, on the reasoning that `hookBudget` hands an overrunning hook's unused SLICE to the hooks behind it — true, but the grace is added ON TOP of each hook's deadline and is never charged against the phase's remaining time, so only the slice is conserved and each stalled hook cost a further full grace. The overrun therefore grew with hook count (1.0/1.4/2.4/3.4/3.7 graces at 1/3/6/9/10 stalled hooks), and on the shipped constants the full sequence measured **59.2s against the 51s the gate computes and the 60s compose grace — one further durable closer took it to 60.0s**. The cross-artifact gate could not see it because it validates the `Total + 2×grace` FORMULA, not the behaviour; the behaviour is now pinned in hook count by `shutdown_envelope_test.go` (7 gates: three regression, verified failing against the original per-hook grace, plus TWO controls that fail against the clamp-only shape (durable closers must have COMPLETED when `RunAll` returns, and each late hook's execution window must clear `shutdownHookMinSlice/4` — a merely-positive assertion passed against the defect, since an expired ctx returns in tens of ns), a healthy-path control, and an unbounded-phase boundary). `runShutdownHook` also waits for the hook goroutine to signal ENTRY before arming the watchdog, so an already-due watchdog cannot race the runtime's scheduling of the goroutine. **Do not re-arm the grace per hook, and do not widen `Total` or `stop_grace_period` instead — that hides the symptom and leaves the bound uncomputable from the constants.** **Do not collapse this back to one deadline per phase.** The first shipped shape did, with the recorded rationale "a stalled hook is abandoned, and the flush hooks are safe because they have their own reserved phase" — which is FALSE: the flush reserve protects flush hooks from a stuck DRAIN, never from EACH OTHER, so `syslog-close`/`community-db-close` stalling on a wedged volume burned the whole reserve and `request-log-close`/`audit-log-close`/`log-closer` were started and abandoned against an already-past deadline (Codex P1 — SD-2b reproduced inside the fix for SD-2b). The hook RECEIVES the deadline the watchdog enforces, so a ctx-aware hook winds down instead of being abandoned; a fast hook hands its unused share to the next, so a legitimately slow close still gets almost the whole phase (control-gated). An abandoned hook is NAMED in a line emitted AT THE POINT OF ABANDONMENT, never from the phase-end aggregate: the last flush hook closes the log sink, so a phase-end line on the flush phase is enqueued into a channel nobody drains (the same reason `runShutdownSequence` emits its completion line BEFORE the flush phase — the pre-existing `logger.Println("Stopped.")` after it was already being swallowed). Abandoning is never worse than the SIGKILL it replaces, which abandons the same hook PLUS everything behind it. **(4) Panics are CONTAINED** — same direction as CHAOS-55's recovery loop, opposite to CHAOS-24's HA keepalive, for the reason recorded there: a shutdown hook holds no authority containment would extend. **(5) `StopControlPlaneGRPC` is bounded** (`gracefulStopBounded`, `cpGRPCGracefulStopBudget` 8s — above the measured 6.0s idle drain so an unresponsive fleet still completes gracefully), then a force-close; force-closing is safe because an interrupted unary RPC is retried by the caller's own sync loop. **grpc-go's `stop(graceful bool)` is hostile to being raced in TWO ways and the obvious wrapper hits both — do not "simplify" this back.** (a) It ends with `s.handlersWG.Wait()`, so `GracefulStop` does not return until every HANDLER returns — the very handler being escaped — so the goroutine must NOT be joined (draft one did). (b) That `handlersWG.Wait()` runs while HOLDING `s.mu` (`stop` takes the lock with `defer Unlock` before the conns wait; `s.cv.Wait()` releases it only while waiting), so when the last conn is removed both stops contend and, if the GRACEFUL one wins, it parks forever holding the mutex and a SYNCHRONOUS `srv.Stop()` blocks on it unboundedly — the original fault rebuilt inside its own fix. Which one wins is pure timing: draft two passed every targeted run AND the full suite and failed only under `-race`. So `Stop()` is issued on its OWN goroutine and the function returns. What it therefore guarantees is narrower than "the server is stopped": the LISTENERS are closed (grpc-go closes them before the conns wait), and the transport force-close is best-effort + asynchronous — which is why the hook watchdog is the only HARD bound on this hook and the two are NOT redundant. The gate's TOLERANCE is part of the gate: a generous "returns eventually" bound would have made draft two a flake rather than a failure. **(6) A SECOND SIGTERM/SIGINT force-exits** with status 1 after `flushLogSink()` — `signal.Notify` takes the runtime's default terminate behaviour away and nothing read `quit` again, so the second signal was swallowed and the only escalation left was SIGKILL. The watcher re-checks the disarm via `shouldEscalate` before exiting: Go picks UNIFORMLY among ready select cases, so a signal pending at the instant `stopEscalation` ran made a COMPLETED shutdown exit 1 half the time (Codex P2). That tie cannot be scheduled from a test, so the decision is its own function and is pinned deterministically rather than by a probabilistic race gate. Gates: `shutdown_chaos_test.go` (17) — four defect gates verified failing against their reintroduced pre-fix shapes, a permanent DEFECT PROOF (`BareGracefulStopIsUnboundedOnAWedgedStream`) so a future grpc-go change cannot let the bounded gate quietly prove less than it claims, and three CONTROLS (a watchdog that abandoned everything, or a drain clamped to nothing, would pass the defect gates while being far worse than the defect). Deliberately left: no unclean-shutdown breadcrumb (the one signal a SIGKILL cannot destroy — recorded as SD-5), no shutdown metrics (a process that is exiting is never scraped again; the log is the record, which is only true because the envelope now guarantees the flush), and `HAState.Stop()`'s `wg.Wait()` stays an unbounded join now covered by the early phase's watchdog. See `roadmap/CHAOS-ENGINEERING-REVIEW.md` §24 and `docs/operator/graceful-shutdown.md`.

## The SOCKS5 accept loop backs off, classifies, and reports (CHAOS-54, `socks5.go` `serve` + `socks5_health.go`)

[Original lines 178–178](https://github.com/KidCarmi/Culvert/blob/3fcc07e7ab5d1e3bd4d19e6cadc8bb63b682f3af/CLAUDE.md#L178-L178) · `claude-main-L178-L178`

<!-- BEGIN preserved-block: claude-main-L178-L178 -->
- **The SOCKS5 accept loop backs off, classifies, and reports (CHAOS-54, `socks5.go` `serve` + `socks5_health.go`)**: `socks5Server.serve` is the ONLY hand-rolled accept loop in the data plane — the proxy port, admin UI, PAC endpoint and MCP gateway all run under `net/http.Server.Serve` and the control plane under gRPC, both of which back off on accept failure and stop on an unrecoverable error. This loop did neither: any `Accept` error other than `net.ErrClosed` was logged and retried IMMEDIATELY, forever. `accept(2)` returns EMFILE/ENFILE straight out of `internal/poll.FD.Accept` without blocking (only EINTR/ECONNABORTED are retried in the runtime, only EAGAIN waits), so a descriptor incident measured **7,681,156 accept attempts in 300 ms** with one `logger.Printf` each — a pinned core, ~40 MB/s into a `fileutil.RotatingFile` capped at 50 MB with ONE archive (so the entire retained process log, INCLUDING the cause of the exhaustion, was overwritten in ~2 s), and — because `internal/logsink` BLOCKS a producer on a full queue and `handleRequest` writes one POLICY_* line per proxied request through that same sink — added latency to every HTTP request on a node whose SOCKS5 listener nobody was using. Self-amplifying: FD exhaustion is the recorded terminal state of WK-11 (leaked alert sockets) and PX-6 (no global connection cap). Four rules now hold and must not be relaxed. **(1) Backoff is net/http's exact schedule** (5 ms doubling to a 1 s ceiling, `nextSOCKS5AcceptBackoff`), reset on an OBSERVED successful accept — copied rather than invented because it is the schedule every other listener in this process already follows, and its ceiling is deliberately below the 2 s `socks5-listener-stop` shutdown budget. **(2) The sleep is INTERRUPTIBLE**: `Stop` closes a `stopping` channel (via `sync.Once`) BEFORE `ln.Close()`, so shutdown never waits out a backoff; measured worst case at the ceiling 107 µs, pinned by `TestChaos54_StopIsPromptDuringAcceptBackoff` (a many-trial gate — where `Stop` lands inside a non-interruptible sleep is uniform, so one trial passes a broken build a quarter of the time). **(3) Only a socket that is GONE stops the loop**: `socks5AcceptFatal` matches EBADF/ENOTSOCK/EINVAL/EFAULT/ENOTCONN via `errors.As` (never string matching — net wraps as `*net.OpError{*os.SyscallError{syscall.Errno}}` and the text is platform-specific), closes the listener so clients get connection-refused instead of hanging against a bound-but-never-accepting port, and records DOWN. An UNRECOGNISED error is deliberately NOT fatal — backed off to 1/s, retrying an unknown error costs nothing, while misclassifying a transient fault as fatal is a customer-visible outage; that does not violate "avoid infinite retries" because the retry is never silent. **(4) Degradation is a DURATION, not a count** (`socks5AcceptDegradedAfter` 30 s): the ceiling is reached in ~1.3 s and paging on that would page on every transient spike. Logging is rate-limited (onset immediately, then ≤1 line per 30 s, then one recovery line naming the suppressed count — signal in the log, magnitude in the counter) and the loop carries a panic guard that reports DOWN (the CHAOS-24 objection to recovering in a worker goroutine does not apply when the recovery path is the loudest state the subsystem can produce). Recovery clears ONLY on an observed successful accept, never on elapsed time. **Degraded and down carry SEPARATE fire-once latches** (`alerted` vs `downAlerted`) — a shared one swallowed the page for a dead listener whenever it had already been degraded, silencing the more urgent of two states that point at opposite actions (storage_health.go's "two failures must not share a rate gate" rule). Surfaces: `socks5_listener` operator-contract row, report-only `/readyz socks5` row (absent when unconfigured; FIXED detail strings — `/readyz` is unauthenticated on the proxy port and an error count + reason would fingerprint a resource-exhausted node), `/healthz socks5` field, `culvert_socks5_{listener_up,accept_errors_total,accept_degraded,accept_backoff_seconds}` (emitted only when configured — `up 0` on a node that never had SOCKS5 is indistinguishable from a dead listener and the paging rule is `== 0`), and a fire-once-per-episode `socks5_listener_down` alert whose Detail carries a BOUNDED reason class (`Dispatch` dedups on `event + ":" + Detail`; a raw accept error embeds the listener address — the WK-12/RS-5 defect). **`net.ErrClosed` is NOT by itself a shutdown signal** — it says the listener is gone, and `Stop` is only one way that happens; the loop checks whether `stopping` was actually closed (`Stop` closes it BEFORE `ln.Close()`, so the check is race-free in the direction that matters) and otherwise records DOWN, because returning silently would leave every probe green on a dead listener — PX-18 in miniature, reintroduced inside the change that closed it (Codex review, PR #1208). Gates: `socks5_accept_chaos_test.go` (18). Still open on this path and deliberately untouched: SOCKS5 never consults the policy engine (no category/GeoIP/schedule rules, no default-deny), plus PX-1/PX-5/PX-6/PX-7/PX-8. See `roadmap/CHAOS-ENGINEERING-REVIEW.md` §22 and `docs/operator/socks5-listener-health.md`.

## An OPTIONAL listener may never terminate the primary one (CHAOS-66, `socks5_bind.go` + `socks5_health.go`)

[Original lines 179–179](https://github.com/KidCarmi/Culvert/blob/3fcc07e7ab5d1e3bd4d19e6cadc8bb63b682f3af/CLAUDE.md#L179-L179) · `claude-main-L179-L179`

<!-- BEGIN preserved-block: claude-main-L179-L179 -->
- **An OPTIONAL listener may never terminate the primary one (CHAOS-66, `socks5_bind.go` + `socks5_health.go`)**: `startSOCKS5` bound with exactly one error branch — `logFatalf`, which `os.Exit(1)`s the process — so EVERY way the optional SOCKS5 listener could fail to bind terminated the whole appliance. It did so from `initSOCKS5`, which main.go runs **BEFORE `startAdminUI` and `buildAndStartProxyServer`**, so the HTTP/HTTPS proxy and the admin UI never started at all. This is CHAOS-57's finding (§33) one plane over and it lands strictly harder: there the MANAGEMENT plane killed the DATA plane; here a SECONDARY, opt-in data plane (`-socks5-port 0` by default) kills the PRIMARY data plane, the management plane and the health endpoints before any of them exist. Reproduced against the real binary with port 11080 held: `SOCKS5 listen error: ... address already in use`, **exit 1**, `proxy http_code=000`, and ZERO admin-UI log lines. The triggers are ROUTINE and none is visible to `validatePortCollisions`, which compares Culvert's own three ports to EACH OTHER only: a predecessor container still draining, a host-network service, a second Culvert (`port_in_use`); a privileged port on a deployment that dropped `CAP_NET_BIND_SERVICE` or stopped running as root (`permission_denied`); binding before the interface is up (`address_unavailable`). Under `restart: unless-stopped` each is an unattended CRASH LOOP recoverable only with shell access. **"It exits, so it fails closed" is wrong and must not be re-argued** — §33's rule: process death picks NO posture, it delegates the choice to the topology (an explicit-proxy fleet loses all egress; a PAC/WPAD fleet with a `DIRECT` fallback, or a transparent deployment, goes UNFILTERED). A `socks5Supervisor` now owns bind → serve → rebind, borrowing CHAOS-54/55/57's mechanism wholesale rather than inventing a second dialect; **`socks5Server` is byte-identical**, which is what lets the lifecycle be added without disturbing the semantics CHAOS-54's 18 accept-loop gates pin. Five rules: no bind path is fatal; retry is RATE-bounded and never COUNT-bounded (1 s doubling to 30 s, ±20% jitter — "avoid infinite retries" is satisfied the CHAOS-54/55 way, by the retry never being SILENT); recovery is declared on OBSERVED evidence only (a listener that actually bound); the sleep is INTERRUPTIBLE so the 2 s `socks5-listener-stop` budget is never spent waiting out a backoff; and reason classes are BOUNDED, matched via `errors.As` on `syscall.Errno` and never by string. Three decisions worth keeping: **`noteSOCKS5Configured` moved BEFORE the first bind attempt** (it gates every SOCKS5 surface, so recording it after a successful bind reports a listener that has NEVER come up as "SOCKS5 not configured" — indistinguishable from an appliance that never asked for SOCKS5, on exactly the node where an operator is debugging it; the same reasoning CHAOS-54 applied one step later); **an observed bind CLEARS the accept plane's `down`** (CHAOS-54 recorded it as terminal, correct only while nothing re-opened the socket — a fresh socket IS the recovery, and leaving it latched would report a fail row, `listener_up 0` and a page after the outage was over), and the `down` row's operator action no longer says "Restart this node", which would now cost a production outage to achieve what happens on its own; and **the backoff is NEVER reset inside the loop** (the `serveAdminUIWithRetry` shape — resetting on every successful bind lets a socket that dies immediately after each bind settle into a permanent one-bind-per-floor cadence, whereas monotonic escalation bounds that pathological case at one attempt per 30 s). **A second, smaller finding rode along**: `classifyAdminUIListenError`'s `network_error` branch was unreachable-by-accident in the other direction — `*net.OpError` satisfies `net.Error` UNCONDITIONALLY (`Timeout()` false for a bind EINVAL), so every unrecognised errno was reported as a network fault, sending an operator down a network-troubleshooting path for a socket or permission fault, and `listen_failed` could only be reached by an error the net package had NOT produced; the shipped gate passed exactly that one shape. Both classifiers now require `ne.Timeout()`. Surfaces reuse the EXISTING vocabulary — no new alert event (a new name is silently unsubscribed on every configured webhook), the same `socks5_listener` row with the bind branches FIRST (a listener with no socket at all is more fundamental, and while unbound the accept fields describe the PREVIOUS socket), the same report-only `/readyz socks5` row with FIXED detail strings, an UNCHANGED `/healthz` enum, and `culvert_socks5_{unavailable,bind_failures_total,binds_total,bind_backoff_seconds}` emitted ONLY when configured; `culvert_socks5_listener_up` extends symmetrically (0 when the accept loop stopped OR the bind has failed past `socks5BindUnavailableAfter` 30 s — a listener merely RETRYING stays 1, so an ordinary redeploy never pages). **A Codex round closed the window the first draft dismissed**: `noteSOCKS5Configured` runs BEFORE the first bind (deliberately — see above), but `startSOCKS5` used to return as soon as `go s.run()` was spawned, so until that goroutine was scheduled `configured` was true with nothing bound and every surface described a listener that does not exist (`ready`, `/readyz ok`, "accepting connections", `listener_up 1`). The window is bounded by the SCHEDULER, not by a syscall, and `main.go` goes straight on to `startAdminUI`/`buildAndStartProxyServer`, so the health endpoints can be reachable before the loop has run. **The fix is to REMOVE the window, not to report it** — a "pending" state would have added a fifth `/healthz` value and still shipped a surface saying "not serving yet" where the honest answer costs one syscall. `startSOCKS5` now waits for the loop to RESOLVE its first attempt (success or failure) before returning — which is what the pre-CHAOS-66 code did, minus the fatal — bounded by one non-blocking `bind(2)`, with `run` closing the handshake channel from a DEFERRED call as well as after each attempt so a panic before the first attempt cannot hang startup. **A SECOND Codex round split the DOWN recorder**: an observed bind clears the accept plane's `down`, and the contract row's operator action was updated accordingly — but the alert Detail and BOTH accept-loop log lines still said "unavailable until restart", so an operator would restart a gateway to achieve what was already in progress (*fixing one surface does not fix the call*, the SOCKS5 log-injection rule one level up). **A blanket reword would have been wrong in the other direction**: the supervisor's OWN contained panic is still terminal — nothing rebinds — so promising an automatic rebind there sends the operator away from the one restart that IS needed. Hence `noteSOCKS5ListenerDown` (accept plane, rebind pending) vs `noteSOCKS5SupervisorDown` (terminal), two named functions rather than a bool so the call site states which it means, with `downRecoveryPending` carrying it to the contract row and the alert's outlook clause branching on it (Detail stays BOUNDED in both directions — it is the dedup key). A gate on the RECORDERS was insufficient and mutation testing proved it: swapping which recorder the panic guard calls left both behavioural subtests green, so the WIRING is pinned structurally (each plane's file calls its own recorder, never the other's). **A THIRD Codex round closed the outage CLOCK and the mismatched remedy.** Every episode duration was `lastFailure - firstFailure`, so it FROZE the instant an attempt returned: at the 30 s ceiling with ±20% jitter a failure landing at 29 s left `/healthz` degraded, `culvert_socks5_listener_up` at 1, the contract row saying "retrying" and the page unfired for up to 36 s AFTER the documented threshold elapsed — a duration derived from two stored stamps needs no clock and is therefore frozen between them, which is why the READ path had no clock at all while every `note*` function took its `now` from the caller. This is CHAOS-61's *freshness is EVALUATED, never latched* in a second place, and it needs BOTH halves: `socks5ElapsedSince` (via the `socks5HealthNow` seam) ages an episode against the clock, which fixes every READ surface — but NOT the alert, which is produced by an ATTEMPT (`noteSOCKS5BindFailure` holds the fire-once latch and nothing else wakes the loop), so `clampSOCKS5BindSleep` also shortens the one sleep that would straddle the threshold (CHAOS-55's `recoveryPollCeiling` rule: an interval that can straddle a state transition is capped below it, a CORRECTNESS bound, at most one extra attempt per episode — capping the CEILING instead was rejected as paying permanently for a property that matters on one sleep). `socks5ElapsedSince` takes the LATER of the stored and clock-derived ends so a clock rollback cannot shrink an outage already observed — deliberately the OPPOSITE of CHAOS-61's rollback verdict, because there the fail-safe answer is distrusting a remote value and here it is the longer duration. The ACCEPT plane had the identical shape with a 1 s ceiling (~1 s exposure) and is fixed in the SAME line rather than recorded: two dialects for one question inside one struct is the trap. Second finding: the contract row printed ONE operator action for every reason class, so a node out of descriptors or with an interface not yet up was sent to hunt the owner of a port nobody holds — `socks5BindRemedy` now selects per class (two clauses invariant in every branch: the listener rebinds by itself, and the proxy/admin UI are unaffected; the unrecognised classes point at the log line). *A bounded classifier is worth nothing if one remedy is printed for every class* — the CHAOS-57 "the evidence must match the claim" family. **That fix then broke the DETERMINISM gate, and the lesson outlasts it: a health snapshot must be a pure function of recorded state and an INJECTED clock.** Giving the READ path a clock while every gate drives the WRITE path with synthetic stamps MIXES two clocks, and `socks5ElapsedSince`'s max() lets the real one dominate — so a gate recording failures 19 s apart synthetically and asserting "not yet degraded" was invisibly also asserting that under 30 s of WALL time passed between two of its own statements (true in milliseconds locally, false on a shared runner under `-count=2`). **What made it diagnosable is that it did NOT reproduce**: re-running the whole package under CI's own printed shuffle seed passed, which rules out ORDERING — the thing a determinism failure looks like — and leaves the environment; it then reproduces on demand (remove the freeze, insert 31 s of wall time between the recording and the assertion ⇒ `a 5s burst of bind failures was reported as unavailable`; restore the freeze and the identical delay passes). `socks5ChaosSetup` therefore FREEZES the read clock at test start, restoring the pre-round-3 semantics for every existing gate while making them deterministic, and a gate that wants an episode to age advances the injected clock EXPLICITLY as the condition under test. `TestChaos66_HealthSnapshotDependsOnlyOnTheInjectedClock` is the wall (holds the clock still across real elapsed time and requires the duration not to move, THEN advances the injected clock alone and requires that it does — the second half is not optional, or a read path ignoring the clock entirely would pass). Gates: `socks5_bind_chaos_test.go` (33), fifteen mutations each confirmed failing their gate, plus a CONTROL requiring the four diagnosable classes to carry DISTINCT remedies (a switch returning one string satisfies every "names its own remedy" assertion otherwise) and a STRUCTURAL wall scanning the three listener sources for `logFatalf`. **`main.go`'s `logFatalf("Proxy error")` is deliberately UNCHANGED and correct** — the proxy IS the product, and a gateway that cannot serve must exit loudly rather than linger as a black hole; that asymmetry is the whole finding. ## The admin plane may never terminate the data plane (CHAOS-57, `ui.go` `serveAdminUIWithRetry`/`adminUIServeOnce` + `admin_ui_health.go`)

[Original lines 180–180](https://github.com/KidCarmi/Culvert/blob/3fcc07e7ab5d1e3bd4d19e6cadc8bb63b682f3af/CLAUDE.md#L180-L180) · `claude-main-L180-L180`

<!-- BEGIN preserved-block: claude-main-L180-L180 -->
- **The admin plane may never terminate the data plane (CHAOS-57, `ui.go` `serveAdminUIWithRetry`/`adminUIServeOnce` + `admin_ui_health.go`)**: `startUI` spawned a detached listen goroutine whose ONLY error branch was `logFatalf` — which `os.Exit(1)`s the process — so **every way the ADMIN UI's listener could fail terminated the PROXY DATA PLANE with it**. `startUI` runs in the init block BEFORE the proxy listener starts and returns as soon as the goroutine is spawned, so the death lands asynchronously against a process already announcing itself as up. Two triggers, both reproduced against the real binary and both ROUTINE operations: an occupied admin port (`validatePortCollisions` compares only Culvert's own three ports to EACH OTHER — nothing on the host is visible to it) and an unreadable custom UI certificate (`ListenAndServeTLS` reads `-tls-cert`/`-tls-key` at call time, so a certbot/cert-manager/Docker-secret rotation that momentarily truncates or re-permissions the pair is a boot that ends in exit 1). Under `restart: unless-stopped` each is an unattended CRASH LOOP — no proxy, no admin UI, no health endpoint, shell access only — the same outcome CHAOS-50 §19 closed for the category store, reached from a busy TCP port instead of a damaged file. **"It exits, so it fails closed" is wrong and must not be re-argued: process death picks NO posture** — it delegates the choice to the topology (an explicit-proxy fleet loses all egress; a PAC/WPAD fleet with a `DIRECT` fallback, or a transparent deployment that bypasses a dead next hop, goes UNFILTERED). Eight rules now hold. **(1) No listen path is fatal.** **(2) The bind is EXPLICIT** (`net.Listen`, not `ListenAndServe`) so the loop can tell a successful bind from a serve that ended — which is what makes evidence-based recovery implementable at all. **(3) Retry is RATE-bounded, never COUNT-bounded** (1 s doubling to 30 s, ±20% jitter via the shared `jitterDuration`, interruptible via `haSleepInterruptible`): unbounded attempts are deliberate on CHAOS-55's reasoning — the terminal state of "give up" is an appliance nobody can manage — and "avoid infinite retries" is satisfied the CHAOS-54/55 way, by the retry never being SILENT (onset logged immediately, then ≤1 line per 60 s, then a recovery line naming the suppressed count). **(4) The certificate is re-read on EVERY attempt**, so a rotation that momentarily breaks the pair self-heals with no restart. **(5) The pair is validated BEFORE the listener is bound** — `http.Server.ServeTLS` returns a certificate error WITHOUT closing the listener it was handed, so a bind-first loop would leak one socket per attempt against a persistently bad cert, turning a config fault into the descriptor exhaustion CHAOS-54 exists for (pinned by a 50-attempt FD-count gate). **(6) The success log moved to AFTER the bind** — the pre-change line printed `UIHTTP: http://localhost:%d` before the attempt, so the log claimed a listener that never existed. **(7) Recovery is declared on OBSERVED evidence only** (a listener that actually bound); elapsed time never clears the state, because a loop that stopped failing because it stopped attempting looks identical to a bound one. **(8) Every surface rides the PROXY port** — `/health admin_ui` (fixed 5-value enum), report-only `/ready admin_ui`, `culvert_admin_ui_{up,unavailable,listen_failures_total,binds_total,listen_backoff_seconds}`, the `admin_ui_listener` contract row and a fire-once `admin_ui_unavailable` alert stating that traffic is unaffected — because the admin port's own `/healthz` cannot report that the admin port is unreachable. **The `/ready` row is REPORT-ONLY and that is load-bearing** (pinned as a control test): a node whose admin UI cannot bind is proxying perfectly, so failing readiness would eject a healthy gateway from the load balancer over its management plane, converting a management outage into the traffic outage the change exists to prevent; strict callers opt in via `?strict=1`. Unavailability is a DURATION (30 s), not a count, so an ordinary redeploy in which a predecessor is still draining never pages. Reason classes are BOUNDED (`port_in_use`, `permission_denied`, `address_unavailable`, `descriptors_exhausted`, `tls_certificate`, `network_error`, `listen_failed`) and matched via `errors.As` on `syscall.Errno`, never by string — the raw error reaches the rate-limited log and nowhere else (`/health` and `/ready` are unauthenticated on the proxy port, and an unbounded reason gives the alert dedup key one value per failure — the WK-12/RS-5 defect). **`runProxyUntilShutdown`'s `logFatalf("Proxy error")` is deliberately UNCHANGED and correct** — the proxy IS the product, and a gateway that cannot serve must exit loudly rather than linger as a black hole. Gates: `admin_ui_listener_chaos_test.go` (15); the defect gates were verified failing against the reintroduced pre-fix shape, where `logFatalf` kills the TEST BINARY mid-run and takes the whole package with it, so the defect cannot be reintroduced and kept green. Still open and recorded: `startSOCKS5`'s BIND failure is still fatal (an occupied SOCKS5 port takes down HTTP proxying — same class one plane over, but a posture decision), plus R-F (the three data-file boot loads). See `roadmap/CHAOS-ENGINEERING-REVIEW.md` §25 and `docs/operator/admin-ui-listener-recovery.md`.

## Every hijacked tunnel is registered with the shutdown drain, and the drain can END the wait (CHAOS-57, `proxy_tunnel_drain.go`)

[Original lines 203–203](https://github.com/KidCarmi/Culvert/blob/3fcc07e7ab5d1e3bd4d19e6cadc8bb63b682f3af/CLAUDE.md#L203-L203) · `claude-main-L203-L203`

<!-- BEGIN preserved-block: claude-main-L203-L203 -->
- **Every hijacked tunnel is registered with the shutdown drain, and the drain can END the wait (CHAOS-57, `proxy_tunnel_drain.go`)**: `drainActiveTunnels` waits on ONE number — `activeConns` — and Culvert has SEVEN hijacked-tunnel classes. Four never touched it: both non-TLS inspect fallbacks (strip + native), WebSocket, and SOCKS5. A hijacked conn is invisible to `http.Server.Shutdown` BY CONSTRUCTION, and `socks5Server.Stop` waits only for the ACCEPT LOOP while every session runs in a detached `go handleSOCKS5(conn)` (a deferral `socks5_shutdown_test.go`'s own header records). So for those four classes nothing waited and nothing closed them — they ran until process exit and the kernel reset them. Three silent consequences: **one fault, two postures** (a CONNECT tunnel got 15 s of grace on SIGTERM; a WebSocket or SSH-over-SOCKS5 session on the same node at the same instant got none, decided only by which `recordActiveConn` call site the path happened to pass); **the accounting for every severed tunnel was destroyed** (`recordTunnelClose*` runs only AFTER both relay goroutines drain, so every graceful shutdown dropped the bytes + duration of every in-flight raw tunnel from the request log, the JSONL export, the SIEM feed and the dashboard totals — systematic across a rolling fleet upgrade); and **the drain's own evidence undercounted** ("Draining 0 active tunnel(s)" on a node severing hundreds of sessions; `activeConns` is also the dashboard field an operator sizes FD budgets from). **Counting alone would have been the WRONG fix and this is the load-bearing part**: long-lived is what WebSocket and SOCKS5 are FOR, so a drain that waits on them with no way to END the wait hits its deadline on EVERY shutdown of a node carrying one — a guaranteed 15 s per node across a fleet upgrade — and severs them anyway; the operator pays the window and gets nothing. So the registry holds BOTH legs and the drain deadline force-closes them (mirroring PR3d's `forceCloseH2InspectTunnels` rather than inventing a second mechanism): each relay's `io.Copy` returns, its parent runs `recordTunnelClose*`, and the accounting lands while the FLUSH hooks (order ≥ 110) are still ahead. Force-closing is never worse than the SIGKILL it replaces — that abandons the same conns AND the accounting. Five rules hold. **(1) `registerDrainableTunnel` OWNS the `activeConns` accounting for its class** — a caller must NOT also call `recordActiveConn`, or the drain waits on a count that never reaches zero and spends the whole window on every shutdown forever after; release is idempotent (`sync.Once`), because a negative gauge makes `active <= 0` true permanently and restores the original blindness by accident. **(2) The map key is the ENTRY POINTER, never a conn**: two tunnels can legitimately hold the same conn value (the strip-path fallback registers `rawClient` while the inspect path registers the `tls.Conn` wrapping it), and a conn-keyed registry lets one release evict the other's registration — measured, it evicts BOTH. **(3) The backstop closes CONNS, not contexts** — `idleCopyCounted` sits in `io.CopyBuffer`, which returns only on a read/write error, and the peer direction may be parked in a deadline-less Write nothing but a close can end; entries are LEFT in the map (the relay's own release removes them; deleting here races a concurrent release into a double decrement). **(4) The settle is a CEILING clamped to the phase budget** (`tunnelForceCloseSettle`, 2 s) and is SKIPPED on the `ctx.Done()` branch — the budget is already spent there, lingering would take time from the hooks behind us, and losing that accounting is exactly the pre-change behaviour, never worse. **(5) An inspected-H2 tunnel appears in BOTH registries** and that is deliberate, not double-counting: this registry owns the single `activeConns` increment while `culvert_h2_inspect_active` measures the GOAWAY-capable subset; both backstops fire at the same deadline and a second `Close` is a no-op. Surfaces: `culvert_tunnels_active{class}` (five classes; the label names are a monitoring contract, pinned by test) + `culvert_tunnel_drain_forced_total`, and a per-class breakdown in the drain's log line — when the drain times out, WHICH kind of session held the node decides the remedy. A **PX-4 residual** closed alongside: `relayPlaintextInspectFallback` was the ONE relay goroutine in the tree with no panic guard (every other raw relay has carried one since CHAOS-24), so a panic there killed an in-line security appliance and dropped every OTHER in-flight tunnel with it. Gates: `proxy_tunnel_drain_chaos_test.go` (15) — every defect gate verified failing against its reintroduced pre-fix shape, plus CONTROLS for the two cheapest wrong fixes (force-closing at drain START passes every defect gate while being strictly worse than the defect; a quiet node must still shut down instantly). **A SHUTDOWN FENCE (`fenceTunnelEstablishment`, hook order 94) refuses NEW long-lived tunnels before the drain looks, and the ordering IS the correctness argument** — after the listeners stop (80/90) so a session the drain could still cover is never refused needlessly, before the drain (100) so nothing establishes behind its back. It exists because **the HTTP paths have a synchronization barrier before the drain and SOCKS5 has none**: `proxySrv.Shutdown` (90) waits for every in-flight request, so a CONNECT/WS either completes or hijacks-and-registers before the drain; `socks5Server.Stop` (80) waits only for the ACCEPT LOOP (each session is a detached `go handleSOCKS5(conn)`), so a session accepted just before Stop can sit in its 30s negotiation deadline plus a 10s dial and register up to ~40s later. Since `drainActiveTunnels` returns IMMEDIATELY when `activeConns <= 0` — exactly a node whose SOCKS5 sessions are all still negotiating — the drain, the backstop and the FLUSH hooks could all complete before the handler sent `0x00 success` and established a tunnel nothing would ever close or account for: PX-8 surviving inside its own fix (Codex review, PR #1288). The fix REFUSES rather than registering earlier: registering at handler entry would make the drain wait on sessions that may never become tunnels and would redefine `culvert_tunnels_active` from live tunnels to attempts, whereas a `0x01` reply is protocol-correct and lets the client retry against another node instead of receiving a success and a tunnel that dies seconds later. The check sits AFTER the dial and immediately BEFORE the reply, so the check-to-register window is microseconds, not a 10s dial. `culvert_tunnel_fence_refused_total` counts refusals; `resetTunnelDrainRegistryForTest` must clear the fence (a leaked fence refuses every SOCKS5 session in every later test). Deliberately left: `go trackDestinationCountry` still unguarded, and tunnels are cut rather than migrated — draining a node before a restart stays the operator's job. See `roadmap/CHAOS-ENGINEERING-REVIEW.md` §25 and `docs/operator/tunnel-drain-on-shutdown.md`.
